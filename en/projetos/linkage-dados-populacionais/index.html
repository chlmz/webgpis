We link longitudinal data and administrative records to broaden the study of health trajectories with security, governance and data protection.

What does linkage mean?

Linkage means connecting records about the same person across different sources, following security and governance rules.

In a cohort, much of the information comes from interviews and follow-ups carried out over time. These data are essential, but they do not capture everything that happens between one visit and the next.

Where there is authorised institutional access, administrative records can complement this story with information on visits, hospitalisations, diagnoses, service use and other events over the course of life.

What kinds of records can contribute?

The project considers different types of public records, always depending on institutional authorisation, availability and a defined scientific purpose.

Health and hospitalisations

Records of visits, admissions and other events related to the use of health services.

Health surveillance

Information produced by official notification and monitoring systems for health conditions.

Social and environmental context

Records that help study exposures and events in the local area, such as states of emergency or violence.

Future trajectories

Where there is authorised access, information from other public sectors can help broaden the study of long-term outcomes.

How are two sources connected?

Prepare

Sources are organised according to rules defined in advance for the project and for each institution involved.

Compare

Information available in the records is compared using linkage procedures suited to the origin and quality of each database.

Review

Uncertain or inconsistent matches are handled conservatively and may be classified as non-matches.

Pseudonymise

After linkage, the data used in analyses are separated from direct identifiers whenever possible.

Identity and analysis stay separate

Linkage is only useful if participant protection is part of the method from the start.

Personal identifiers are handled in a restricted environment, with access limited to the authorised team. After linkage, the data used for analysis are pseudonymised.

The key that links identifiers to research codes is kept separately, protected and encrypted.

Inconsistent matches are not forced: when there is not enough evidence that two records belong to the same person, they are treated as non-matches.

Different methods for different sources

Larger databases may require probabilistic linkage procedures, while local records may allow assisted or manual review under institutional supervision.

The choice depends on the structure, quality and availability of the data. The goal is to increase accuracy without lowering participant protection standards.

Extending the 2019 Rio Grande Cohort

The 2019 Rio Grande Cohort follows mothers, children and families from birth. Linkage makes it possible to extend this story by connecting, securely and with authorisation, information collected directly by the research to records produced by public services and systems.

This can help study events that happen between follow-ups and observe trajectories over longer periods without relying solely on new interviews.
